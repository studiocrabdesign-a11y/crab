Carpet area, built-up area, super built-up area — what's the difference?

Every apartment in India has three different area figures attached to it, and builders don't always make it obvious which one they're quoting. Carpet area is the space you actually live in — the usable floor area inside your walls. Built-up area adds the thickness of those walls plus any balcony. Super built-up area (or saleable area) adds a share of the building's shared spaces — lobbies, stairwells, lifts, sometimes the clubhouse.

Since RERA came into force, builders are required to quote carpet area alongside the sale price, but the headline rate per square foot is still usually pegged to super built-up area. Two flats priced identically per square foot can differ by hundreds of usable square feet depending on the loading factor — the gap between what you're quoted and what you can actually furnish.

What is carpet area?

The net usable floor area inside an apartment's walls — literally the area you could lay a carpet across. Under RERA it excludes external wall thickness but includes internal partition walls.

What is built-up area?

Carpet area plus the thickness of the apartment's own walls and any attached balcony or terrace. Typically 10–20% larger than carpet area.

What is super built-up area?

Built-up area plus a proportionate share of common areas like lobbies, staircases, lifts and clubhouses. This is usually what the quoted price-per-square-foot is based on, and is typically 20–35% larger than carpet area.

Why does the difference matter when buying a flat?

You're priced on super built-up area but you only live in the carpet area. Always ask for the carpet area figure before comparing two properties on price per square foot.
