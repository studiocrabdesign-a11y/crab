It's the first question almost every client wants answered and the last one most designers want to commit to in writing, because the honest answer is a range, not a number. In Bengaluru, as a rough guide, essential fit-outs — clean, functional, built around modular pieces — run somewhere between ₹1,200 and ₹1,800 per square foot of carpet area. Refined interiors, with more considered materials and some custom joinery, sit between ₹1,800 and ₹3,000. Fully bespoke work, where almost nothing is off-the-shelf, starts around ₹3,000 and climbs well past ₹5,000 depending on how far you push it.

Three things move a project between those bands. The first is finish level: modular, factory-made carpentry costs a fraction of solid-wood or stone custom work, and this single decision affects the budget more than any other. The second is scope — a partial refresh of one or two rooms costs far less per square foot than a full gut renovation or fitting out a bare shell from scratch, since site preparation and structural work carry their own overhead regardless of finish. The third is simply which part of the city you're building in; rates in central, established neighbourhoods tend to run higher than in newer developments further out, largely on labour and logistics.

What these ranges can't capture is everything specific to your actual space: whether walls need to move, what condition the existing electrical and plumbing are in, what grade of appliances and fittings you're specifying. Two 1,500 square foot apartments can land two or three times apart in final cost once those specifics are accounted for.

A calculator can get you to a ballpark in under a minute. Getting from ballpark to an actual number takes a site visit and a conversation about what you actually want the space to do.
