Modular vs Custom Interiors — Where the Budget Actually Goes

Ask where an interiors budget actually goes and most people guess area — bigger home, bigger bill. Area matters, but it's rarely the biggest lever in the room. The decision that moves a budget the most is how much of the space is modular versus custom-built, and the gap between the two is larger than most first-time renovators expect.

Modular furniture and cabinetry are manufactured in standard sizes, assembled from factory-finished panels, and installed rather than built on site. That standardisation is exactly what keeps the cost down — the same components are used across thousands of homes, so the price per unit reflects volume manufacturing, not one-off craftsmanship. For kitchens and wardrobes especially, modular systems have become good enough in finish and durability that the case for going fully custom has narrowed considerably.

Custom joinery is a different proposition entirely. Solid wood, stone tops, site-built carcasses, finishes applied by hand — every piece is made once, for one space, by someone paid for their time rather than for output volume. That's where the craftsmanship shows, and it's also where the cost multiplies. A custom wardrobe wall can cost two to three times its modular equivalent for a comparable footprint.

The pragmatic approach most experienced designers land on is neither purely modular nor purely custom — it's custom where it's seen and felt most (a feature wall, a statement piece, an entrance) and modular where consistency and cost efficiency matter more (storage, utility spaces, repeated elements). Deciding that split early, before drawings are finalised, is the single most effective way to keep a budget under control without the space feeling generic.

How much does interior design cost per square foot in India?

Roughly: essential/basic fit-outs run ₹1,200-1,800/sq ft, mid-range refined interiors ₹1,800-3,000/sq ft, and premium bespoke work ₹3,000-5,000+/sq ft. Rates vary by city, vendor and materials.

What affects the cost the most?

Finish level has the biggest impact, followed by city, followed by project type — new build vs. full renovation vs. partial renovation.
